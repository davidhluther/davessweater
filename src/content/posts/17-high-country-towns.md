# Dave's Sweater Now Tracks 17 High Country Towns

For four months this site graded every forecast anyone published for one town. As of this week it
does the same thing for eighteen: Boone plus 17 more places across Watauga, Ashe, Avery, Mitchell,
Wilkes, and Yancey counties.

## What a town gets

Each town now has its own page with a multi-source forecast pulled at that town's actual
coordinates, not a regional blend with the town's name stamped on top. [Beech
Mountain](/weather/beech-mountain) gets forecasts computed for 5,436 feet. [North
Wilkesboro](/weather/north-wilkesboro) gets forecasts computed for 1,001 feet. Those are two
different forecasting problems, 4,435 feet apart, and the numbers say so: on July 25 the forecast
highs for the two towns sat 13 degrees apart before lunch, with Beech in the upper 60s and North
Wilkesboro at 80.

Every town is also graded. The same 100-point rubric that has scored Boone forecasts since March
now runs against each town's own verified conditions, every day. A town's scoreboard appears once
it has nine scored days behind it, which is the same bar we made every forecaster clear before
ranking them. Nothing gets ranked on a hunch.

## The honesty rules came along

Two things we refused to do while multiplying. First, no borrowed coordinates: every town's pin was
geocode-verified before a single forecast was pulled, because a forecast for the wrong valley is
worse than no forecast. Second, no borrowed stations. Ray's Weather publishes town numbers for most
of this set, and where he genuinely has a station in a community, his column is there to be graded.
Where he doesn't, in Seven Devils, Sugar Grove, and Wilkesboro, his column sits empty. We would
rather show you a blank than dress up the next town over as coverage.

## What we don't know yet

Whether elevation makes a town harder to forecast is now an answerable question. The ridge towns
and the river towns are being graded side by side for the first time, and in about a month the
data will say which forecasters handle the vertical better. We have a guess. We are not going to
publish a guess.

The [methodology](/methodology) is the same one, in public, that it has always been. Pick your
town, check the scoreboard, and if you live somewhere we skipped, tell us and we will run the
numbers on adding it.
