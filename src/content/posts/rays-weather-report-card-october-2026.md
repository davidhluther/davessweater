# Ray's Weather Report Card: October 2026

We scored 31 days of Boone forecasts in October 2026. Open-Meteo averaged 92.9 and Ray's Weather 74.6, an 18.3-point gap, the widest since June. Ray's month landed just under the 75-point line that separates Right from Meh, the first time that has happened since June.

## Key takeaways

- Ray's Weather averaged 74.6 in October 2026, a Meh month on average, and 18.3 points behind free Open-Meteo
- Ray's went 18-9-4, his most Wrong days in a month since June; Open-Meteo went 31-0-0
- Ray's outscored Open-Meteo on two days, October 13 and October 19, and trailed on the other 29
- Wind cost Ray's more than any other field, 7.0 points a day, and he scored zero on wind five times
- Ray's worst day, October 12 at 46.3, was the lowest grade any source posted all month, on a day Open-Meteo scored a perfect 100

## How did Ray's Weather score in October 2026?

Ray's Weather averaged 74.6 across 31 scored days in October 2026, which is Meh territory by 0.4 of a point. He posted 18 Right days, 9 Meh days, and 4 Wrong days. Open-Meteo never left Right territory.

That 0.4 matters more than its size suggests. July, August, and September all finished as Right months for Ray's, if narrowly, and October broke the run. June, at 69.7, was the last month he averaged below 75.

We capture each source's forecast around midday and grade it the next day against verified actuals from the Open-Meteo historical archive. The monthly score is the mean of the daily 0 to 100 grades. October had 31 clean days for every source.

### October 2026 at a glance

October cooled hard in the back half. The average high was 69.6°F over the first 15 days and 61.9°F over the last 16, and the month as a whole averaged 65.6°F by day and 47.4°F at night. It was also dry, with measurable rain on just 9 of 31 days, against 19 in September. One storm did most of the work. October 10 brought 2.24 inches of the month's 5.63, and no day reached freezing at the grid point we grade against (the coldest night was 33.4°F on October 18).

## Which Boone forecast was most accurate in October 2026?

MET Norway was the most accurate Boone forecast in October at 94.1, ahead of Open-Meteo at 92.9 and WeatherAPI at 91.6. Ray's Weather was ninth of nine, and the next-lowest source, Visual Crossing at 85.9, finished 11.3 points above him.

### What does the R-M-W column mean?

Right is 75 or above, Meh is 60 to 74, and Wrong is anything under 60. The cold front of October 16 and 17 dragged most of the table down for a day or two, yet only two sources ended any October day Wrong. OpenWeather did it once, with a 57.9 on October 16. Ray's did it four times.

## What were Ray's best and worst days in October?

Ray's Weather ranged from 46.3 on October 12 to 91.2 on October 18, a 44.9-point swing. His four Wrong days all fell between October 10 and October 17, one bad week inside a month that was otherwise mostly Right.

### What was Ray's worst October day?

October 12 was Ray's worst at 46.3, and Open-Meteo nailed the same day for a perfect 100. Ray's forecast a high of 76°F. Boone topped out at 67.6°F, 8.4 degrees under, which left him 7.8 of 30 points on the high. He called for wind of 5 to 15 mph and the day averaged 19.7, which zeroed the wind field. A "no rain" call on a day with 0.07 inches of drizzle cost him four more.

The other three Wrong days share a shape. On October 10 (57.1), the 2.24-inch storm, Ray's got the high right to within a degree and correctly called rain. His wind forecast of 10 to 50 mph scored zero, though, and a rain call without an amount can earn only half the precipitation points. On October 16 (52.3) and October 17 (54.7) the highs were again close or exact, and the misses came from the overnight low and from wind ranges of 5 to 20 mph against days that blew harder than their midpoints.

### What was Ray's best October day?

October 18 was Ray's best at 91.2, the clear, cold morning after the front. He forecast 56°F and 32°F against actuals of 52.8°F and 33.4°F, wind of 5 to 10 mph against 8.5, and a dry day that stayed dry. Open-Meteo still scored 92.9. Ray's two wins were narrow ones, 86.9 to 85.1 on October 13 and 89.2 to 88.6 on October 19.

## Why did wind cost Ray's so much?

October's 18.3-point gap came from all four fields, and wind was the biggest piece by a distance. Ray's averaged 12.3 of 20 on wind against Open-Meteo's 19.3, a 7.0-point daily hole. The overnight low came next at 5.3 points (21.9 of 30 against 27.2), then the high at 3.6 (23.9 against 27.5) and precipitation at 2.4 (16.5 against 18.9).

The wind number has a mechanical cause worth spelling out, because part of it comes from how we score and part from how Ray's forecasts. Ray's publishes wind as a range, and our rubric scores a range at its midpoint, then charges for its width. A 5 to 20 mph call is graded as 12.5 mph plus a vagueness penalty, so October 16, which averaged 18.2 mph, scored zero on wind even though it sat inside the range Ray's printed. In a calm month that rarely bites. October's fronts made it bite five times. The full rule is on the [methodology page](/methodology), and you can judge for yourself whether a 15-mph-wide range ought to earn full marks.

The low is the other thing to watch. Ray's average miss on the overnight low was 3.7°F in October, up from 2.4°F in September, while Open-Meteo held at 1.8°F. That field has drifted the wrong way two months running.

### Where the points go

Each forecast is graded out of 100, split across four fields:

- High temp: 30
- Low temp: 30
- Wind: 20
- Precip: 20

## What does an 18-point monthly gap actually mean?

It means the gap is widening again. Month by month since July it has gone 13.2, 14.7, 16.0, and now 18.3, closing in on June's 21.2. Week by week, Open-Meteo led in every block of October, by as much as 22.1 points (October 8 to 14) and as little as 13.5 (October 22 to 28). Ray's best stretch was the last ten days, when the weather settled and he averaged 80.4.

Across July through October, 122 days, Ray's averaged 76.0 and Open-Meteo 91.6, and Ray's came out ahead on 6 of those days. Since Ray's first scored day on March 5, across 239 days, Open-Meteo averages 90.8 and Ray's 71.7 on the same days.

All of this measures one-day-out forecasts. It doesn't speak to [seven- or ten-day lead time](/resources/articles/how-accurate-is-a-10-day-forecast), so this card can't say how Ray's does at that range. The case for why we track any of it is in [is Ray's Weather accurate?](/resources/articles/is-rays-weather-accurate), and the month before this one is the [September 2026 report card](/report-card/2026-09).
